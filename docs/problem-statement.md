# Problem Statement

## Background

The logistics and supply-chain industry depends on the reliable movement of shipments through complex networks of roads, ports, warehouses, carriers, trucks, containers, and vessels. Disruptions such as extreme weather, port strikes, geopolitical crises, route closures, and equipment failures can quickly affect multiple shipments and create cascading delays.

At the same time, fleet assets may remain underutilized in one location while other routes experience capacity shortages. Cold-chain shipments such as vaccines, medicines, and perishable goods face an additional risk because temperature deviations can damage cargo before operations teams are aware of the problem.

## The Problem

Supply-chain operations teams lack a unified, intelligent way to identify how disruptions affect active shipments and determine the best corrective actions.

When a disruption occurs, teams must manually examine shipment information, disruption data, carrier availability, fleet capacity, routes, and cold-chain sensor readings. This makes it difficult to quickly identify affected shipments, find alternative routes or carriers, redeploy idle fleet assets, and detect temperature excursions before cargo is damaged.

As a result, disruptions can cascade into shipment delays, inefficient fleet utilization, increased transportation costs, and potential loss of high-value temperature-sensitive cargo.

## Who is Affected

The primary users are supply-chain and logistics operations teams responsible for monitoring shipments, managing transportation disruptions, allocating fleet assets, and responding to cold-chain incidents.

The problem also affects:

Fleet managers who need to identify and redeploy idle trucks, containers, and vessels.
Logistics planners who need alternative routes and carrier recommendations during disruptions.
Cold-chain operators responsible for temperature-sensitive shipments.
Warehouse and transportation teams coordinating affected shipments.
Customers and businesses that depend on timely and safe delivery of goods.
## Why It Matters

The impact of supply-chain disruptions can be significant. Delayed shipments can cause missed delivery commitments, increased transportation and storage costs, and inefficient use of fleet capacity.

Cold-chain failures are particularly costly because temperature-sensitive products such as vaccines, medicines, and perishables can become unusable after a temperature excursion. A single incident can potentially result in the loss of cargo worth $500K or more.

Early detection and intelligent recommendations can therefore help organizations:

Reduce shipment delays.
Improve fleet utilization.
Reduce unnecessary transportation costs.
Detect cold-chain risks before delivery.
Prioritize high-severity incidents.
Give operations teams faster, data-driven actions.
## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Existing logistics systems often provide individual pieces of information—such as shipment tracking, fleet status, route information, or IoT temperature readings—but do not combine these signals into a single decision-making workflow.

Operations teams may need to manually compare multiple datasets and systems to determine which shipments are affected, which assets are available, which alternative routes or carriers should be used, and whether a cold-chain incident requires immediate action.

This fragmented approach increases response time and makes it difficult to understand the overall impact of a disruption. The proposed Supply Chain Disruption Assistant & Fleet Utilisation Optimizer addresses this gap by combining disruption analysis, shipment impact mapping, route and carrier recommendations, idle fleet matching, cold-chain monitoring, severity classification, and AI-generated explanations into one workflow.
